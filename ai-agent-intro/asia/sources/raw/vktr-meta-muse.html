Meta Launches Muse, a Personal AI Agent That Browses the Web, Makes Purchases and Works While You Sleep

Meta's Muse agent runs on its own secure VM, browses the web, fills out forms and keeps working after you close the app.

Key Takeaways

  • Meta launched Muse, a personal AI agent that handles tasks autonomously, including browsing the web, filling out forms, making purchases and continuing to work after you close the app.
  • Each user gets a dedicated virtual machine in the cloud with a separate Sentinel agent that must approve anything before it reaches the internet.
  • Meta settled an $18 billion multistate lawsuit over consumer harms less than two weeks ago. Muse requires more personal data than any Meta product before it.

Meta on Tuesday launched Muse, a personal AI agent that can complete tasks on a user's behalf, including shopping, booking travel, sending emails, filling out forms and working toward long-term goals. The agent is available now in the US on iOS, Android and muse.ai, with WhatsApp integration and AI glasses support coming later.

Powered by Muse Spark, Meta's flagship model, Muse is free for most tasks, with subscription tiers available for users who want more.

Not a Chatbot. An Agent With a Browser.

Once a user tells Muse their goal, the agent builds a plan, coordinates resources and takes action on its own. It can open a browser, navigate websites, fill out forms and negotiate on the user's behalf. For tasks that take longer, it keeps working after the user closes the app and returns when something changes or when it needs approval, such as before sending an email or making a purchase.

Meta says Muse remembers what matters to a user across conversations. It can turn a recipe saved on Instagram into a grocery list, remember a friend's dietary restrictions before sending dinner invitations and suggest actions unprompted based on details mentioned once in passing.

Payments are handled through Link by Stripe, which generates one-time-use card numbers so that users' actual payment details are never shared with merchants. According to Meta, Muse is the first AI agent covered by Link's purchase protections, including free coverage for damaged or lost items and no-fee returns. Shop Pay integration and 1Password support are coming.

A Dedicated Computer for Every User

As CEO Mark Zuckerberg described it, "Muse is built from the ground up for privacy and security." Each user gets their own Muse Secure VM, a dedicated virtual machine in the cloud with its own browser, storage and compute resources. No other user's agent can reach it.

A separate Sentinel agent runs on the same machine but is isolated from Muse at the system level. Nothing Muse does reaches the internet unless the Sentinel approves it. Sensitive actions — sending messages, making purchases, changing permissions — require explicit user confirmation.

Meta says Muse has no visibility into passwords or payment methods. Credentials go into secure storage and Muse uses them without seeing them, including passwords typed into the browser. Users choose which apps to connect, set the level of access for each and can disconnect any service at any time.

Users can opt out of their interactions being used to train Meta's AI models. Muse conversations and VM data are not shared with Meta's ad systems. Later this year, Meta plans to introduce Muse Confidential VM, where the entire virtual machine — including user data and conversations — is encrypted with a key only the user holds.

Meta also opened its Muse bug bounty program to the public, with awards up to $300,000 for valid reports and up to $130,000 for successful prompt injection attacks that affect a single user.

The Trust Problem Meta Can't Engineer Around

Muse requires more personal trust than any Meta product before it — email access, calendar access, browser activity, purchase history, long-term personal goals. And it launches from a company with one of the worst privacy track records.

Meta agreed to an $18 billion multi-state settlement over consumer harms less than two weeks ago. That followed a $942M judgment in New Mexico over harms to children, a $5B FTC penalty in 2019 and the Cambridge Analytica scandal. Thousands of personal injury and school district cases remain pending.

Meta's safety team was candid about the challenge in its technical blog post: "As soon as we started using it, we saw the glimmers of real personal superintelligence — an agent that knows you, actually does things, works in the background, launches swarms of subagents, builds its own tools, and edits itself. It was also the first time we'd handed our inboxes, our calendars, and a shell to a piece of software and let it run unattended — which didn't always work out as planned. Making the technology work for everyone requires careful design and engineering to operate more safely."
